Learning

Learning by doing, the Montessori way

Our classrooms follow the authentic AMI Montessori curriculum. Children choose meaningful work, repeat it until they master it, and grow in focus, independence and joy.

1907The year Dr Maria Montessori opened her first classroom. Over a century of practice in classrooms worldwide.
AMIAssociation Montessori Internationale, founded by Maria Montessori in 1929 to protect her method. Our directresses are AMI trained.
5Areas of learning in every classroom, from practical life to mathematics, taught with hands-on materials.
Why it works

What the research says

Montessori education has been studied in independent research. Two well-known studies compared children who won a place in a Montessori pre-school through a lottery with similar children who did not.

Published in Science, 2006

Stronger reading, maths and self-control by age 5

Five-year-olds in Montessori classrooms scored higher on early reading and maths, and showed better self-control and social reasoning, than children in other programmes.

Lillard & Else-Quest, Science 313
Frontiers in Psychology, 2017

Bigger gains over three pre-school years

Over three years, children in Montessori pre-schools made greater gains in academic achievement and enjoyed school more. The gap between lower and higher-income children narrowed.

Lillard et al., Front. Psychol. 8
The curriculum

Five areas of learning

Tap an area to see the materials your child will use and the skills it builds.

🧺

Practical life

Real tasks from daily life: pouring, buttoning, washing, sweeping and caring for plants. Our Laundry and Cleaning Days bring it to life.

Materials your child will use
Pouring jugsDressing framesWashing & laundryFood preparationPlant care
What it builds
  • Independence
  • Concentration
  • Fine motor control
  • Care for others
🎨

Sensorial

Beautifully made materials isolate one quality at a time, such as size, colour, texture or sound, so children learn to notice, compare and order.

Materials your child will use
Pink TowerKnobbed CylindersColour TabletsSound BoxesGeometric Solids
What it builds
  • Observation
  • Ordering & sorting
  • Vocabulary
  • Readiness for maths
🔤

Language

Children hear and trace letter sounds before they write, then build words with the Moveable Alphabet. Stories, songs and conversation every day, in English, Sinhala and Tamil.

Materials your child will use
Sandpaper LettersMoveable AlphabetObject-to-picture cardsStory cornerRhymes & songs
What it builds
  • Phonics
  • Early reading
  • Early writing
  • Speaking with confidence
🔢

Mathematics

Numbers are something children can hold. Rods, beads and cards turn quantity and place value into hands-on work long before worksheets.

Materials your child will use
Number RodsSpindle BoxesGolden BeadsTeen & Ten BoardsBead chains
What it builds
  • Counting & quantity
  • Place value
  • Adding & subtracting
  • Problem solving

Culture & the world

Puzzle maps, plants, animals, art and music, plus the festivals of every Sri Lankan community, so children grow curious about the world and respectful of others.

Materials your child will use
Puzzle MapsLand & water formsLife cyclesArt & musicFestival projects

Stage by stage

What your child will be able to do

By the end of each year, most children can: confirm with teachers

2 – 3 years

Playgroup

  • Separates calmly and enjoys the daily routine
  • Pours, spoons and tidies up independently
  • Names colours, shapes and everyday objects
  • Sings rhymes and joins group circle time
3 – 4 years

Lower KG

  • Recognises letter sounds and builds simple words
  • Counts and matches quantities to 10
  • Dresses, serves and cares for the classroom
  • Focuses on a chosen activity for longer
4 – 5 years

Upper KG

  • Reads and writes short words and sentences
  • Works with numbers to 100 and simple sums
  • Speaks confidently in front of others
  • Is ready for Grade 1 admission interviews

Daycare until 5.30 pm confirm

Lunch, rest, quiet play and homework help in a calm, familiar setting, for parents who work full days.

How we track progress

You will always know how your child is doing

Montessori teachers observe closely and record each child's progress across all five areas.
